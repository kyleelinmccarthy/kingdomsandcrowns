# The Realm: Presentation Overhaul — Design Brief

**Date:** 2026-09-10  
**Status:** Draft. Awaiting the decisions in section 6 before any implementation plan is written.  
**Occasion:** After playing slice 8 the user returned a verdict listing eighteen problems, ending "this doesnt feel like a well thought out game at all." This brief is the answer to that verdict.  
**Method:** six parallel code audits (one per complaint cluster, 68 findings), four independent overhaul proposals from different design angles, three judges, one synthesis. Every claim cites code that was read.

## 1. The verdict, verbatim

> the world still looks pretty rough since the buildings are flat and its not clear that the clouds are enemies and the spell bar looks really rough and there are not slots for the mount. theres no way to get to spellbook from here and no quest log or tracking. no starting quest. no main tavern no tutorial. i dont like the location of the mana or player name/health. its really, really rough in a bad way and needs to improve a LOT. there should be an indicator over or under the character and names over NPCs and should be WAY more development in the world. also the castle looks like a TeePee. messages are left aligned rather than centered which is no good. this doesnt feel like a well thought out game at all.

## 2. Diagnosis

The Realm has a genuinely good simulation and a well-built app behind it, and almost none of it reaches the player: the world never says what you are here to do, nothing on screen is anchored to the hero or to the people, and every channel that could carry meaning — silhouette, ground contact, nameplate, marker, slot, message placement, hit reaction, sound — is unused, or pointed at the wrong object. Nine high-contrast labels float over scenery while the eight villagers and every enemy are anonymous; the resource that never gates play gets prime real estate while the states that do (dazzle, cast, shield) are invisible; the only route to the Spellbook disappears the moment the child achieves the goal we set them. All eighteen complaints are one failure at different distances — the child cannot read the screen, so no system underneath it can be enjoyed, and the fix is to rebuild the channels the game speaks through before adding a single new mechanic.

## 3. Direction

Presentation first, judged by a legibility lens: every change has to answer one of "which one is me", "where do I go", "what is that thing", or "what did my work change". We do not add mechanics; we make the existing ones readable, and where the world can do the teaching instead of the HUD (a Tavern you walk into, a keep that grows, a plot that rises, a name over a person) the world wins, because that scales with the reader instead of against them across a 6-to-14 age band. Two constraints govern every decision. The first is the metered clock: play is earned in five-minute grants, so nothing may spend the child's minutes on traversal or onboarding overhead — no zoomed-out world to walk across, no sixty-second tutorial before the first real thing happens, and no arcade obstacle ever placed between a child and their schoolwork. The second is that this is a homeschool tracker, so the payoff loop — deeds done in the app visibly changing the landscape and getting reported back at the close — is the point, not a garnish. Sequencing is load-bearing and non-negotiable in two places: the sprite-sampling fix lands before any art is redrawn (otherwise the redraw is done twice at a 2.7–5.3x point-sampled minification and still looks rough), and the markers land before anything changes the camera. Slice 1 alone is designed to flip the headline verdict; slice 2 fixes "rough" in the mechanical sense; then we stop, the user replays, and the plan is allowed to change. Two standing rules apply to every slice: a same-framing before/after screenshot on the documented port-3100 setup is the acceptance gate (unit tests cannot judge whether something looks like a castle), and every HUD, bar and message change must have a parent-preview path, because `isChildView: false` nulls mana, cleared, ride and minutes and injects a hero selector into the exact row we are rebuilding. Honest total: nine slices, three or four months of part-time work. This is a rebuild of the presentation layer, not a polish pass.

### Slice 2 — Stop looking rough (and build the tool that makes the art possible)  `[medium]`

**Goal:** Fix the mechanical cause of 'rough' before a single figure is redrawn, make the scale relationships sane, and stand up the gallery page that turns the art work from one blind iteration into ten fast ones. Then stop: the user replays and the plan is allowed to change.

- Pixel budget: delete the fixed WORLD_SPRITE_SCALE ({castle:8, building:6, foundation:4, decor:4}) and derive raster scale from CAMERA_ZOOM and the sprite's world size so texel-per-device-pixel is an exact integer. Measured minification today is 2.13x–5.33x with minFilter AND magFilter both NearestFilter and no mip chain, so a 1-unit flag pole lands on 1.88 screen px — 1px one frame, 2px the next. Note world-figures.test.tsx:56 deep-asserts WORLD_SPRITE_SCALE and must move in the same commit.
- Bucket dpr to 1 or 2 instead of [1, 1.5], so the continuous fractional case that guarantees a non-integer ratio disappears and the browser stops bilinearly upscaling a point-sampled scene on retina.
- Ground textures: generateMipmaps with minFilter = LinearMipmapNearestFilter while keeping magFilter Nearest — kills the crawl when the camera follows the hero without softening magnified pixels. Drop #ffffff from GRASS_COLORS and cut flower density: white on dark green is the highest-contrast pair on screen, ~4 per tile, on an exact 2-unit grid repeated 3600 times.
- PREREQUISITE, and the compound bill nobody costed: SpriteSource performs ~34 rasterisations one `await` at a time and disposeSpriteTextures() runs on unmount, so the whole bill is re-paid on every return. Raising raster scale multiplies it — a citadel canvas goes from 512px to well over 1000px. Parallelise the awaits and keep the cache warm across a short round trip (dispose on route change away from /realm, or on a timer) before anything raises the texture budget.
- Scale ladder against the 2-unit hero, held: bush 1, fence 1.2 × 2.4 (wide, as its three-post figure was actually drawn), lantern 2.5, oak 5, pine 6, cottage-class building 4, chapel 6 with a tower to 8, library 5, watchtower 9. Trees are currently SHORTER than the person walking past them and bushes/rocks/fences/lanterns are 0.9 units, under half a person tall. Give each building its own footprint in place of the shared {w:3,d:3,h:2.5}, so a Chapel, a Library and a Market Square stop being the same box.
- Sprite baseline anchoring: set spriteMaterial center to (0.5, 0) and place sprites at the ground point instead of [0, h/2, 0], and offset building sprites to the footprint's near edge so the ~2-unit invisible dead zone in front of every building (collider is half-width + HERO_RADIUS from the centre) gets a visible cause.
- Delete the baked <rect x=4 y=56 width=56 height=4 fill="#24492e"> fake shadow on every castle and building — that colour is literally GRASS_COLORS[2], so it vanishes into the tufts.
- Idle vs walk: track moved = hypot(Δp)/dt with a lastPos ref. Idle bobs 0.02 at 1.5Hz; walking bobs 0.08 at 8Hz with a small alternating sprite.material.rotation step-lean. Give the companion a π phase offset — hero and companion currently pulse in perfect lockstep, which is itself part of why they read as floating. All of it collapses to zero when settings.motion is false.
- NEW: a /dev/figures gallery route rendering every world figure, trouble and castle stage at its true display size against the actual grass colour, side by side, in both palettes, with a size ruler against the 2-unit hero. A couple of hours of work, and it is the difference between one iteration pass and ten on the largest and least verifiable cost in the whole programme.
- CHECKPOINT: ship slices 1 and 2, take same-framing before/after screenshots of the two views the user photographed, and have them play again. Do not start slice 3 until that feedback lands. The plan below is a proposal, not a commitment.

**Resolves:** the world still looks pretty rough; its really, really rough in a bad way and needs to improve a LOT

### Slice 3 — The ability bar and the mount  `[large]`

**Goal:** The bottom of the screen becomes a control surface a child can choose from: fixed-shape slots with the cost always visible, obviously-empty sockets that stop impersonating abilities, the mount finally present as a thing, and a Spellbook button that can never disappear.

- Rebuild the chips as fixed-size square slots (56px × scale) in a fixed-count row so the bar has a stable shape regardless of how long a child names their spell — today they are variable-width text pills in an overflow-x:auto strip whose names are hidden outright below 640px.
- Mana cost bottom-right ALWAYS. It currently renders only after a page is selected (spell-bar.tsx:123), which is backwards: the cost is what you use to choose. Unaffordable becomes a desaturated icon plus a red cost, not a blanket opacity:.55 that hides the number too.
- Empty pages become clearly-empty recessed sockets — dashed inset, no keycap, no icon — instead of the same pill with a dashed border, which is why three of the four things in the user's screenshot look like abilities the hero owns.
- Consume the castState event the shell currently throws away (`case "castState": break;`) to draw a cast-progress sweep on the active slot; castMs runs 300-900ms, long enough to need it.
- Mana moves to hug the top edge of the bar so cost and resource read together, and the 7rem corner meter is gone for good.
- Mount slot after a divider at the right end: the mount's own sprite as a thumbnail (MountFigure is a plain SVG component, so the DOM bar renders it directly with no rasterisation plumbing), keycap M, pressed state while riding. When no mount is equipped, render the slot EMPTY-BUT-PRESENT with a silhouette, and let a tap open a picker over the bar listing bundle.mounts.unlocked. Today hudRide is null entirely when avatarConfig.mount is unset, so a child who earned a Stag at level 8 sees no mount affordance in the world at all. Delete the Ride button from the HUD row.
- Permanent Spellbook button pinned at the bar's right end. Today the only /spellbook link in the open world lives two interactions deep inside a hint that only an EMPTY chip can open — fill all four pages, which is the goal we set, and the Spellbook becomes unreachable.
- One scale knob: set --realm-hud-scale on .realm-root from settings.hudScale and derive every HUD and bar size from it. The HUD scales in em and the bar in absolute px today, so they can never stay matched.
- Publish the bar's measured height as --realm-bar-height the way GameNavBar already publishes --game-navbar-height, and delete the four hand-tuned bottom offsets that currently let the empty-page hint cover the bar below 640px.
- Dim the bar rather than unmounting it when a panel opens, so it stops popping in and out.
- Add the first ≤640px HUD media query the file has ever had — the only realm media query today touches the spell bar.

**Resolves:** the spell bar looks really rough; there are not slots for the mount; theres no way to get to spellbook from here; i dont like the location of the mana or player name/health

### Slice 5 — Enemies you can read, and combat you can feel  `[large]`

**Goal:** The clouds stop being weather, hitting something means something, and the three moments that currently present as the game freezing — winding up a cast, aiming it, and getting hit — become things the child can see.

- SCOPE CORRECTION, verified against the source: 'gentle means deleting the face' is over-generalised. The gentle Shadow already has two eyes; the gentle CursedStone already carries violet (#7c3aed) cracks. Only Fog is genuinely featureless — three grey ellipses at 0.85 opacity — and Fog is the thing in the user's screenshot. So this is ONE redraw plus a shared treatment, not three.
- Fog redrawn as a friendly-looking opponent: a lumpy grey-lilac body with two stubby arm-puffs, big half-closed grumpy eyes, a small frown, violet motes orbiting. Gentle means pouty, sleepy, grumpy — never blank.

Grey is already the decor rock at a near-identical #7d7d7d standing in the same field, white is the grass flower, green is the bush. One rule a child learns once: violet + face + nameplate = zap it. Under lowStimulus keep the face and outline, drop the sparkles.
- Nameplates over live troubles from TROUBLE_COPY's gentleName/monstersName — written, typed, and read by nothing anywhere in the app — styled as a threat plate rather than the neutral black pill the scenery uses. Draw them as canvas-texture sprites, not drei <Html>, so six of them do not add DOM nodes to the frame loop.
- REAL BUG: SpellLayer binds pooled sprite index i to sim.troubles[i] while the kill removes with filter((_, i) => i !== index), so clearing one enemy makes every later one teleport into the previous slot and change species mid-frame — on essentially every kill with two or more alive. Bind slots to trouble ids in a ref Map.
- Hit reaction: add hurtUntil and hurtDir to Trouble; applyHit sets hurtUntil = now+180 and stores the incoming direction; the layer flashes the material toward white, punches scale ~15%, and recoils ~0.25 units. Swap in a cracked second texture when hitsLeft drops, so the 2-HP cursed stone stops looking untouched.
- Death beat: keep the cleared trouble in the array as dyingUntil = now+350 (excluded from hit tests, stepTroubles AND spawnTroubles' per-site occupancy check — miss the last one and a cleared site stops respawning), then squash, spin, fade, a 16-particle burst in its own accent at its position, and a rising '+1'.
- Honest feedback: stop deriving bursts from lastVanished, which records only projectile and area and fires identically on a hit, an out-of-range expiry and a wall — the game's one piece of combat feedback currently carries zero information, and beam and aura hits are completely silent. Collect hits from stepEffects each frame and spark at every real hit point. One change fixes three bugs.
- Cast wind-up: sim.caster.casting is never rendered while realm-scene folds castingRef into `frozen`, so pressing cast stops the hero dead for up to 900ms and then a ball appears from nowhere. Draw a glow orb growing across the wind-up in the spell's colour plus a ground ring that closes, and a target marker on the trouble nearestTroubleOrAhead silently picked.
- Dazzle made visible and made the hero's: tween the blob's 4-unit pushback over ~200ms instead of teleporting it, flash the hero, spin classic dizzy stars above the head for the full 1500ms, add a soft violet screen-edge vignette, and land the notice in the centred lane. Add a status row on the hero's plate for dazzled and shielded.
- NON-NEGOTIABLE: every cue needs a non-motion substitute. The single existing particle is gated on `motion`, so reducedMotion currently erases 100% of combat feedback for exactly the children the setting exists for. Fades, static stars and colour swaps replace shake and spin.
- Reconsider troubles.ts:160, which skips the blob's chase entirely under lowStimulus — combined with the stone's SPEED 0 and the fog's 3-unit tether, calm mode is currently six pieces of furniture.
- RULING to write down: there is no hero health in this game and we are not adding one. What actually happens to the hero is the 1500ms dazzle, and the shield exists. The hero plate carries focus and shield state; the word 'health' gets an answer rather than a bar the user looks for and cannot find.

**Resolves:** its not clear that the clouds are enemies; this doesnt feel like a well thought out game at all; i dont like the location of the mana or player name/health

### Slice 6 — Sound, and a first five minutes that gates on doing  `[medium]`

**Goal:** Ship the feedback channel the settings page already promises parents and currently does not have, and replace the one-shot glossary with an onboarding a non-reader can actually complete inside a metered session.

- A tiny WebAudio cue synth: oscillator plus a short noise burst through a gain envelope, no asset files, no network, a few hundred bytes. Cue table in a pure testable module (cast, impact, clear, dazzle, talk, deed complete, building rises, gleam, one-minute warning). soundEnabled is already on the learning profile, already persisted in schema, already in the sensory preset, and already shown to parents as 'Sound — Music and effects in the Realm'; a grep for new Audio / AudioContext / playSound across all of src returns literally nothing, so the app is currently advertising a feature it does not have.
- Add `sound` to RenderSettings beside motion/calmPalette/showStick/hudScale, unlock the AudioContext on the first real gesture inside .realm-root, and add a master mute in the world regardless of profile. soundEnabled defaults to true and is independent of lowStimulus, so every cue must be short, soft-attack, low-pitched and quiet.
- New pure src/lib/realm/tutorial.ts + colocated test: a step machine where each step declares its banner copy, its spoken copy, which marker to point at, and a predicate over a small input (distance moved, reachId, panelOpened, deedFinished, troubleNear) that advances it. Onboarding becomes testable without three.js.
- Steps gate on the child DOING, never on reading and clicking Close: welcome → move → walk to the beacon → talk → play one side quest → come back and see the site → meet one trouble → cast. Hold the spell bar and the first trouble spawn until their own steps. Copy branches on settings.showStick exactly as helpGroups(touch) already does.
- Persist tutorialStep on realm_settings beside helpSeenAt (one nullable integer, a Drizzle migration, db:generate then db:migrate — verify the migration landed rather than trusting the hook silently). Play is metered: a child may only have five earned minutes, so a tutorial that cannot survive being interrupted by the clock is not a tutorial.
- Default an absent tutorialStep to 'complete' for any hero whose helpSeenAt is already set, so this never re-locks a returning child.
- Pause the play clock for the opening banner only. Every later step runs on the live clock, and the whole sequence is budgeted to well under a minute — 15% of a child's daily allowance is the ceiling.
- Repoint the '?' button at step one; keep RealmHelp behind it as the control reference. Delete 'Clear troubles to protect the sites' — nothing a trouble does can touch a site, a building or a deed, so the help card currently teaches something false.

### Slice 7 — The kingdom answers the work  `[large]`

**Goal:** Every completed deed changes the landscape permanently and visibly, the world's centrepiece stops being a tent, and the eight identical dark diamonds become plots you can read from across the field.

- Foundation stages driven by done/total: keep the flat dirt quad as a base but stand real content on it — rope and stakes at 0, a timber stack at 1-2, a course of stone at 3-4, a roofed frame under scaffold at 4/5. Five drawn states per site. Today an unbuilt site is a planeGeometry lying flat at y=0.04, which under the 35.26° camera projects to exactly the dark diamond the user photographed, and five hours of work looks pixel-identical to zero.
- Lighten the dirt from #6b665a/#7a7464 to a warm ochre so a plot separates from #2e5a3a grass — there is currently no hue contrast at all.
- Replace the nine floating DOM label pills with in-world wooden signboard sprites carrying the building name and the same pip row the objective card uses. Those white-on-black 11px pills are the highest-contrast objects in both screenshots — they ARE the composition — and deleting them fixes legibility, fixes 'rough', and removes nine <Html> nodes from the frame loop in one move.
- Drive the keep off kingdom progress: a pure keepStageFor(buildingsComplete) with five stages from a scaffolded stone keep with a banner (never a tent) through curtain wall, gatehouse and towers to a citadel at 8 of 8, footprint growing 6→12. Today castleType comes from the cosmetic /castle page gated at levelRequired 50, so a child sees a 120px teepee from level 1 to level 55 and building the entire kingdom changes it by zero pixels. Verified safe: CastleFigure is imported only by sprite-source and its own test; /castle renders GameIcon with CASTLE_ICONS, not these SVGs. Re-run ceremonyMarks against the new footprints and rewrite ceremony.test.ts, which iterates CASTLE_FOOTPRINTS.
- Promote one src/lib/realm/palette.ts for ground, props, accents and shadow and make every figure draw from it, so hue separation from the grass is checkable in one place. Repaint the garden away from green-on-green (#5aa55a/#3d8a4a against #2e5a3a). Delete BUILDING_COLORS' current role — it looks like the world palette but only ever renders in the box-fallback failure path.
- A band of water tiles crossing z≈0 with the bridge slot on it, so the River Bridge stops being a bridge with its own painted blue puddle in a dry field — and the river breaks the empty lawn in half for free.
- Move DECOR_SPOTS inward: 10 of the 12 sit at |x| ≥ 13.5, on the rim, outside the corridor where anything happens. Add per-instance rotation and scale jitter — the two oaks are currently pixel-identical.

## 6. Open questions — decisions only you can make

### Q1. THE CASTLE, AND WHETHER WE RETIRE AN UPGRADE PATH

Driving the keep off kingdom progress is what makes building the eight buildings visible — but CASTLE_TYPES is a cosmetic ladder on the /castle page gated at levels 50-100, and a child may be saving toward a tier. Three options: (a) progress drives the silhouette and the cosmetic tier becomes a skin layered on top, (b) progress drives it and the /castle ladder is retired, (c) leave the castle cosmetic and find another way to show kingdom progress. We recommend (a), but this quietly changes something one of your children may be working toward, so it is your call, not a refactor to slip in.

### Q2. ART DIRECTION, AND A REFERENCE

Slice 8 is roughly 13 hand-authored SVG figures plus 5 foundation stages plus the Tavern and gate — the largest and least testable cost in the programme, and it is worth doing once. Before it starts we need you to confirm the direction (3/4 isometric pixel art, one baked upper-left sun, warm palette) and ideally point at a game or a picture whose look you want. The /dev/figures gallery in Slice 2 exists so you can look at candidates side by side and say yes or no fast.

### Q3. HOW MUCH OF THE WORLD TO BUILD

We have deliberately scoped OUT the full village rewrite — named districts, a road network with spurs to every door, a paved market plaza, 60-100 seeded prop instances. It is the most exciting version and it is also the one the engineering read says will starve enemy spawns, wedge the hero on new walls, and push the sprite rasterisation past 60 on a path that already blocks first paint. What we are building instead is a Tavern, a gate, walls, a river, plots that rise, and a keep that grows. Is that enough 'WAY more development', or do you want the village, accepting that it is roughly another two large slices and pushes everything else back?

### Q4. WHAT IS THE MOUNT FOR? We are giving it a slot, a picker and a sprite, which makes it legible — but you can cross this world in about ten seconds, so an eight-tier mount ladder with speeds from 4.2 to 7.0 against a hero's 3.5 is mechanically close to meaningless, and a slot for it just makes the emptiness legible

Do you want riding to be cosmetic (present it as a pet, not a vehicle), or should it get a job — fast travel between the gate and the far sites, a recess lap advantage, reaching somewhere you cannot walk? The same question applies to the companion, which currently renders, follows, and does nothing.

### Q5. SHOULD COMBAT HAVE STAKES, AND WHERE IS THE LINE? Today a trouble cannot harm a site, a building, a villager or the kingdom — the worst it does is a 1.5-second dazzle — while the help card promises 'clear troubles to protect the sites'

The obvious fix is to let a trouble block its site's side quest, which would make the copy true and weld the arcade half to the learning half. We are recommending AGAINST it: this is a homeschool tracker, and putting an arcade obstacle between a child and their maths practice locks out a kid on a bad motor day (and calm mode, where the only mobile enemy goes passive). The safer stake is that clearing troubles EARNS Realm minutes or restores a dimmed site display. Your call on which, or whether combat stays decorative for now.

### Q6. DO WE CUT ANYTHING? Every proposal on the table adds and none subtracts, and you told us the game is already 'really, really rough'

The candidate for deletion is recess — roughly 384 lines across seven files producing three HUD chips of per-visit state that resets whenever the child navigates and is never persisted. Cutting it concentrates the work and removes a whole surface from the redesign. Keep it, cut it, or park it behind a setting?

### Q7. ONE REALM OR TWO? The app is for roughly 6 to 14

This brief is tuned for the youngest — pips instead of numbers, one objective at a time, a bobbing gold marker, a guided first five minutes. A thirteen-year-old may find that patronising and simply not come back. The cheap answer is that the simplified view is what you get until the tutorial completes; the honest answer is that the Realm has one difficulty, no depth and no mastery at any age. Do you want a complexity axis, or is the youngest child the target and the older ones get the Quest Log?

### Q8. SIBLINGS

There are multiple heroes in this house — the hero-switcher floats over the game board today — and none of them can see each other's kingdoms. 'Nasrin raised the Mill yesterday' on the Tavern board, or a visitable sibling realm, is cheaper than a river and is probably a stronger reason to open this a second time than anything else in this brief. Is that in scope?

### Q9. THE CHILD'S NAME ON THE WORK

We want the finished building to carry 'Raised by Nasrin · Spring 2026', and where it is cheap, what they actually did. It is the strongest emotional payoff available and it costs almost nothing — but it also permanently stamps a child's name into the world in a way that matters if heroes are ever renamed, retired, or shared between siblings. Confirm you want it.

### Cluster: WORLD ART AND LEGIBILITY

**"the world still looks pretty rough"** — _blocking, medium effort_

Every world sprite is point-sampled at roughly a third to a fifth of its authored raster size, with NearestFilter and no mipmaps. Figures are drawn on a 64x64 SVG grid, rasterised at WORLD_SPRITE_SCALE (castle 8 -> 512px, building 6 -> 384px, foundation/decor 4 -> 256px), then displayed at CAMERA_ZOOM 40 on sprites 3 to 3.5 units wide = 120-140 CSS px. Measured minification: castle 4.27x, building 2.74x, foundation 2.13x, oak 5.33x, hero 3.60x. sprite-texture.ts sets magFilter AND minFilter to NearestFilter, so minification is raw point sampling with no mip chain. The result is that a 64-grid source pixel lands on 1.88 screen px (0.75 for an oak) -- a non-integer, so a 1-unit flag pole (world-figures.tsx:45 width={1}) is 1 px on one frame and 2 px on the next, and 3-px merlon teeth come out uneven. This is the mechanical cause of "rough": the pixel grid is destroyed at display time. Canvas dpr is also capped at [1,1.5] (realm-scene.tsx:394), so on a 2x display the browser then bilinearly upscales the point-sampled result.

*Where:* `src/lib/realm/sprite-texture.ts:233-234, src/lib/realm/tile-texture.ts:77-78, src/components/realm/world-figures.tsx:13, src/lib/realm/camera.ts:50, src/components/realm/realm-scene.tsx:394`

## 8. Appendix B — the four proposals and how they scored

### The six-year-old test: design backwards from the youngest player in the family — a non-reader on a trackpad or tablet who has never seen a HUD — and treat every one of the user's complaints as the same failure, legibility.

**Thesis:** The Realm is illegible to the child it is actually for: nothing on screen answers "which one is me", "where do I go", or "what is that thing", so the fix is not more features but three visual answers — a marker on the hero, one gold arrow to one objective at a time, and enemies with faces and names — delivered icon-first, tappable, spoken aloud, with the fake scoreboard in the corner deleted.

**First five minutes under this proposal:** She taps the Realm medallion. The world fades in on her hero standing on cobbles at the south gate, and there is a soft gold ring on the ground around him with a little notch pointing up the road — that is her. A dark oval sits under his feet, so when he bounces he is bouncing on grass, not floating over it. Centre screen, in big letters and read aloud in the same breath: "Welcome to your Realm, Nora. Let's build the Village Well." Nothing else is on screen — no bar, no meters, no chips.\n\nShe drags a finger. Her hero moves; the banner changes to "Follow the arrow" and a fat gold chevron starts bobbing over a dirt patch off to the left, with a small gold arrow stuck to the left edge of the screen because the patch is just out of frame. Under the chevron, standing beside the patch, is a person with a plate over his head reading "Old Bram" and a bright gold "!" bobbing above it. Nobody else in the field has an "!" — the other seven have nothing, so there is exactly one place to go. Top centre, a small card shows a bucket icon and five circles, one filled: ● ○ ○ ○ ○.\n\nShe taps Old Bram directly, because that is what you do. Her hero walks over on his own. When he arrives the "!" pops, a compact "Talk" button appears at 56px, and the panel opens with one side quest already highlighted and its Begin button focused. She plays it — eight questions, the part of the app that already works.\n\nShe comes back out and the world has changed. A course of stone has risen on the dirt patch, the signboard beside it now shows two filled circles, the objective card ticks to ● ● ○ ○ ○, and a single centred line fades in above the ability bar: "Village Well — 2 of 5." Old Bram's plate says "Village Well · 2 of 5."\n\nThen the bar slides up from the bottom for the first time with one square slot lit — her starter spell, its icon filling the slot, its cost printed in the corner, an obviously-empty dashed socket beside it, a divider, and a mount slot with a horse in it. The banner says "Something is in the way." A lumpy grey-lilac cloud with half-shut grumpy eyes and three violet sparkles drifts in near the well, casting its own little shadow, with a red-edged plate over it reading "Fog." She taps the slot, taps the fog, it flashes white, squashes, and pops into violet sparks. Nothing about that sequence required her to read a word, press a key, or find anything in a corner.

### GAME LOOP FIRST — the Realm has no game in it, only a place. Every art complaint is downstream of a loop the child cannot perceive, so the loop gets built before anything is repainted.

**Thesis:** A child spawns in a field holding eight identical dirt diamonds with no objective, no tracker, no marker, and no visible consequence for the forty side quests the kingdom actually costs — so the fix is to give the world one objective at a time, point at it, track it, change the world when it is met, and end the session with a report; repainting buildings before that is polishing a loop nobody can see.

### THE WORLD AS A PLACE — rebuild the Realm as a village you walk through, so the world does the teaching the HUD is currently failing to do.

**Thesis:** The Realm isn't a game missing HUD features; it's a diorama missing a village — 44 props on 1600 square units of lawn, framed by a camera that shows more world than exists, with every "place" replaced by an 11px DOM label — and the fix is to build actual places (a Tavern you walk into, a keep that grows with your work, staked construction sites, districts with roads and a river, doors to the Spellbook and Quest Log) so "what is this, what do I do, where do I go, how do I get out" get answered by looking instead of by chrome.

**First five minutes under this proposal:** The screen opens on a lamp-lit inn door. The hero is standing on cobbles under a hanging sign, a wooden gate arch behind them and a town wall running east and west so the world has a back. North, a cobbled road climbs past a bell tower to a stone keep under scaffolding on the ridge — the biggest thing on screen and clearly a castle, clearly unfinished. There is no tutorial modal.

She walks (any direction works; the road is the obvious one). Left of the inn door a notice board glows faintly; stepping into it pops a bubble — "Read the board · Enter". She presses it and gets eight rows: "Kingdom — 0 of 8 raised", the Village Well at the top with an empty bar. She closes it. That took nine seconds and nobody told her to do it.

Fifty paces up the road there's a fork with a signpost. West says Riverside. She goes west because the road goes there, and it drops down to a plank bridge over actual moving water. On the near bank: a roped-off plot with scaffold poles, a stack of timber, one course of grey stone laid, and a wooden sign facing the road — "Village Well · 0 of 5". A man in a blue tunic stands beside it under a nameplate reading Old Bram, with a gold "!" bobbing over his head.

But there's something over the plot: a grey-lilac cloud with half-closed grumpy eyes, a small frown and three violet sparkles orbiting it. It has a shadow. It is obviously not weather. Bram says he can't work with that about. She taps spell 1, taps the cloud; it flashes white, squashes, and bursts into violet motes with a "+1" floating up. Bram's "!" brightens. She talks to him, plays eight questions, and comes back out to a toast reading "Village Well — 1 of 5" — and the plot she is standing next to now has two courses of stone where it had one.

Up the hill she passes a library with lit windows and an open door. She walks into the doorway out of curiosity and the Spellbook opens. Five minutes in, she has found the quest board, the well, a villager, an enemy, a side quest, and the Spellbook, and not one of those was a button in the corner of the screen.

### Presentation first. The simulation under this game is honestly good — movement slides along walls, the caster has wind-up and release, troubles wander and sense and push back, effects hit and expire, mana regenerates, the ceremony choreographs villagers, recess times laps. Almost none of it reaches the player's eyes or ears. My claim is that every complaint in the user's verdict is a *reading* failure, not a systems failure: the clouds are enemies but are drawn as weather; the buildings are modelled but rasterised at a fifth of their authored resolution and pasted on grass with no shadow; the mount has eight tiers and real speeds but lives as a text button between two score chips; the messages exist but are left-aligned bands in a corner. A player who cannot read the screen cannot enjoy any system, however well built — so I would not add a single new mechanic. I would rebuild the presentation layer end to end: pixel budget, ground contact, silhouette and scale, HUD zones, ability slots, nameplates and markers, enemy legibility, hit and death feedback, message placement, and the sound the settings page already promises.

**Thesis:** Nothing in the Realm is broken except the channels through which it speaks — resolution, silhouette, ground contact, nameplate, marker, slot, message placement, hit reaction, sound — and rebuilding those channels, without touching a single mechanic, is what turns the existing simulation into a game a nine-year-old can read.

**What all of them missed:** NOBODY RULES ON WHAT 'HEALTH' MEANS. The user wrote 'player name/health'. There is no health in this game — no HP, no hearts, no damage; the only thing that happens to the hero is a 1500ms dazzle. All four relocate the name and the mana bar; not one decides whether the hero should HAVE health, and not one says to the user 'there is no health, here is what we're giving you instead and why'. Whichever plan ships, the user will look for the bar they named and not find it. This needs an explicit ruling — add a small hero resource, or state plainly that dazzle-plus-shield is the model and make the status row on the hero nameplate carry it. NOBODY SCHEDULES A SECOND PLAY SESSION. Every proposal is a 7-8 slice plan validated by a firstHourOfPlay that its own author wrote. The entire brief is one person playing for ten minutes and writing a paragraph. The highest-value artifact in this whole exercise is that person playing AGAIN after two slices — not slice 7 — and none of the four builds in a checkpoint where the plan is allowed to change. Given the project already has a documented local screenshot setup on port 3100, a same-framing before/after screenshot pair per slice should be an acceptance gate, not a risk footnote. NOBODY BUILDS ANYTHING FOR THE PARENT — who is the person who wrote the verdict. This is a homeschool tracker; the Realm is the reward layer for schoolwork. Not one proposal adds a surface a parent sees: no Chronicle or Tavern panel showing Realm minutes against side quests finished, no visibility into whether the arcade half is displacing the learning half, no parental control over the new content (a bigger world, faces on enemies, sound). Game-loop-first's session summary is the closest and it is aimed at the child. The user's real acceptance criterion is 'does my kid learn more', and no plan reports on it. NOBODY DOES THE ARITHMETIC ON THE METERED CLOCK. Play is earned in 5-minute grants against a 30-minute daily cap. Three proposals add an onboarding walk, a beacon walk, a Tavern at the far south edge, doors that navigate, and a camera zoomed from 40 to 64-68 so the world is bigger than the screen — every one of which spends the child's earned minutes on traversal instead of on the activity that earns more minutes. A bigger world is more expensive in a metered game. Nobody computes what fraction of a 12-minute session becomes walking, and nobody considers a fast-travel or spawn-at-your-objective answer. EVERYONE ADDS; NOBODY CUTS. Recess is ~384 lines across seven files producing three HUD chips of per-visit state that resets on navigation and is never persisted. The eight cosmetic castle tiers become dead weight the moment the keep is driven by kingdom progress, which three of four propose. The user said the game is 'really, really rough' and every proposal answers with twelve to fourteen additions. A strong plan for a cluttered, unfinished thing deletes a subsystem to concentrate the work — and none of the four proposes cutting anything larger than a HUD chip.
